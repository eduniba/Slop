How the construction works

The sequence is defined by \(a_{n+1}=f(a_n)\) starting from \(a_0\). Mark \(a_0\) on the \(x\)-axis, i.e. the point \((a_0,0)\), and go vertically to the graph of \(f\): you reach \((a_0,f(a_0))=(a_0,a_1)\). Then go horizontally to the diagonal \(y=x\). Since the height is \(a_1\), you land on \((a_1,a_1)\), where the value \(a_1\) is now on the \(x\)-axis: the output has become the next input. Repeating the step from \((a_1,a_1)\) gives \((a_1,a_2)\) and then \((a_2,a_2)\), and so on. The orange path is the broken line \((a_k,a_k)\to(a_k,a_{k+1})\to(a_{k+1},a_{k+1})\), and the labeled points \(a_k\) lie on the diagonal.

A fixed point \(x^{*}\) satisfies \(f(x^{*})=x^{*}\), so it is an intersection of the graph with the diagonal, and an orbit starting there never moves. If \(|f'(x^{*})|<1\) nearby orbits converge to it (green); if \(|f'(x^{*})|>1\) they move away (red); if \(|f'(x^{*})|=1\), as at a tangency, the derivative alone does not decide (purple).
