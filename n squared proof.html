Geometric Insight & Algebraic Induction

1. The L-Shaped Gnomon

In ancient Greek geometry, a gnomon is a piece added to a structure that preserves its overall shape.

Starting with an $n \times n$ square of area $n^2$, to grow it into an $(n+1) \times (n+1)$ square we add a border layer along two adjacent sides.

This layer consists of $n$ units on top, $n$ units on the right, and $1$ corner unit connecting them:

$$n + n + 1 = 2n + 1$$

Because $2n + 1$ is always the $(n+1)$-th odd integer, building a square layer-by-layer naturally sums the sequential odd numbers.

2. Generic Case Identity

When viewing the Generic Case mode, we analyze expanding from size $n \times n$ to $(n+1) \times (n+1)$:

  • Lime Green Core: Area $= n^2$
  • Yellow Strips: Two strips of $n$ unit squares each $= 2n$
  • Magenta Corner: Single $1 \times 1$ square $= 1$

Summing these distinct regions yields the fundamental binomial expansion:

$$n^2 + 2n + 1 = (n+1)^2$$
